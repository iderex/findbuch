# 0006. What the symbolic checker decides, and how it avoids a heuristic verdict

Decided in #7.

## The problem

The symbolic leg is the load-bearing half of the verification, and the way it is
usually written does not work.

The naive implementation computes the bracket and calls the library's general
simplification routine to decide whether the result is zero. That routine is a
heuristic. It returns an unsimplified expression for things that are zero, it
can take unbounded time, and its behaviour changes between releases of the
library.

A verification whose verdict depends on a heuristic is a verification whose
verdict cannot be relied on. Worse, its failures point in the safe-looking
direction: an expression that really is zero but does not simplify reads as a
failed row, and contributors learn to distrust the checker rather than the
transcription.

## The decision

The question is never asked of the heuristic. It is reduced to exact polynomial
identity, which is decidable.

Every Hamiltonian and every integral in the catalogue is a rational function of
the phase space coordinates and the body parameters. The bracket of two such is
again a rational function. Write it over a common denominator, take the
numerator, and expand that numerator into a canonical polynomial form over the
rationals. It is zero if and only if every coefficient is zero, and that is a
comparison of exact integers rather than a judgement.

The library's general simplification routine is not in the verdict path. It may
be used for presentation, for a message a contributor reads, or for anything
else that no verdict depends on. It decides nothing.

## The obligation the denominator creates

The denominator is not discarded.

An identity that holds only where the denominator is nonzero is a statement
about a smaller region than a row claims when it declares its domain. So the
denominator is recorded, and the row's declared domain must exclude its zero
set. A row whose domain does not exclude the poles is refused.

This is the part of the criterion most likely to be dropped as bookkeeping. It
is not bookkeeping. Without it the catalogue would publish rows whose stated
domain is larger than the region the verification covers, which is precisely the
defect the published tables already have.

## Conditional cases and invariant relations

For a conditional case the same reduction happens modulo the ideal generated by
the declared constraints. The numerator is reduced against a Groebner basis of
that ideal, and the criterion is that the remainder is the zero polynomial. This
is exact and it terminates.

For an invariant relation there is no additional integral, and the question is
different: the bracket of the relation with the Hamiltonian must lie in the
ideal generated by the relation itself, which is the statement that the flow
preserves the submanifold.

Parameter conditions that are equalities are substituted before the expansion.
Parameter conditions that are not equalities are carried into the ideal.
Inequalities in the parameters are not part of the symbolic question at all.
They belong to the domain, and the numeric leg is where they matter.

## What this costs

The expansion can be large. A case with several parameters and a quartic
integral produces polynomials with many terms, and that is why the full sweep is
a batch job rather than part of the fast gate. The alternative, which is to be
fast and sometimes wrong, is not an alternative for this project.

## What proves it bites

A mutated row that perturbs one coefficient of a known-good integral must be
refused, and the assertion is that the refusal names that row and no other. A
checker that passes everything and a checker that works look identical on a
corpus of correct rows.
