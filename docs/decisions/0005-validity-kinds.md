# 0005. How a row says what kind of validity it claims

Decided in #6.

## The problem

Several of the best known entries in the published tables are not what the table
header says they are, and a schema without a field for this forces the catalogue
to repeat the error.

Three different claims get written into the same column of a survey table.

A general integral. The bracket of the Hamiltonian with the additional integral
vanishes identically on the whole phase space, subject only to the stated
parameter conditions. Euler, Lagrange and Kovalevskaya are of this kind.

A conditional integral. The bracket vanishes only on a fixed level set of one of
the Casimirs or of another integral. Goryachev-Chaplygin is the standard
example: the fourth integral exists on the zero level of the area constant, and
stating it as a general case is false. Bobylev-Steklov is the same shape with a
different condition.

An invariant relation. There is no additional integral at all. There is a
submanifold that the flow preserves, and on it the problem reduces. Hess is
this, and so are Staude's permanent rotations and Grioli's precession. Recording
an invariant relation in an integral field would make the symbolic checker ask a
question whose true answer is no, and the row would be red for a correct reason
that looks like a wrong one.

## The decision

Every row declares its validity kind, and the symbolic checker asks a different
question for each. The three kinds are `general`, `conditional` and
`invariant-relation`, and there is no fourth.

## The question the checker asks, per kind

For a `general` integral, the checker asks whether the bracket of the
Hamiltonian with the integral is identically zero as a rational expression. That
is decided by exact polynomial identity in the canonical form 0006 fixes, and
never by a simplification heuristic.

For a `conditional` integral, the row declares its constraints, and the checker
asks whether the bracket lies in the ideal generated by them. That is decided by
reduction of the bracket's numerator against a Groebner basis of that ideal, and
the verdict is that the remainder is zero. Deciding it by inspection, or by
substituting the constraint into the expression and simplifying, is what this
record rules out: the first is not a decision procedure and the second answers a
different question.

For an `invariant-relation`, the row declares the relation, and the checker asks
whether the bracket of the relation with the Hamiltonian lies in the ideal
generated by the relation itself. That is the statement that the flow does not
leave the submanifold, and it is decided by the same reduction as the
conditional case, against the ideal the relation generates.

The three questions are different questions, and none of them is a weaker form
of another. A row of one kind that is checked with another kind's question gets
an answer that is about something else.

## The field has no default

A row that does not declare a kind is refused by the schema.

There is no default, and this is the part of the record most likely to be
softened later for convenience. The default would have to be `general`, and the
general case is the one that gets claimed wrongly in the literature this
catalogue is transcribing from. A default would therefore reproduce the exact
error the field exists to catch, on precisely the rows where it matters, and it
would do it silently.

## Why this is worth a field rather than a convention

Because the field is what lets the export tell a consumer the truth. A machine
learning benchmark that trains on trajectories from a conditional case without
knowing the condition will produce a model that learns a constraint nobody told
it about, and the resulting paper will be wrong in a way nobody can debug.

A convention lives in the heads of the people who wrote the rows. A field is
carried into the export, where the consumer is.

## Consequences

The constraints are required exactly when the kind requires them, and refused
when it does not. A `general` row carrying constraints is a row whose author
meant `conditional`, and a `conditional` row carrying none is a row that will be
checked against the empty ideal, which is the general question under a different
name.

The numeric leg reads the kind too. A conditional case has to be integrated from
an initial condition that lies on its constraint, and a numeric run started off
the constraint measures the drift of a quantity that is not conserved there, for
a reason that has nothing to do with the row being wrong.
